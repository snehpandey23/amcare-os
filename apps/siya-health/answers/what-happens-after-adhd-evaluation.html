What happens after an ADHD evaluation?

Educational only: This page is for general education—not personal medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. See a licensed clinician for your situation.

Clinician-informed

Educational content informed by clinical practice patterns—not personal medical advice.

Short answer

After a structured ADHD evaluation, your clinician explains findings in plain language and outlines a care plan. That may include education only, lifestyle supports, therapy referral, labs when indicated, non-stimulant or stimulant options when appropriate—or a recommendation that ADHD is not the best fit for your symptoms. Diagnosis does not guarantee medication.

Detailed answer

Most adults leave with a clearer map: what fits, what still needs ruling out, and what follow-up looks like. Controlled medications are not prescribed during the initial evaluation visit at Siya Health.

If medication is later appropriate, follow-up plans match the treatment path—non-controlled monitoring or controlled-medication follow-up with safety checks per state law. See /pricing for current fees.

Practical next steps often include sleep and medical look-alikes, workplace or school documentation when clinically supported, and coordinating with therapy when executive function or mood needs more than medication alone.

If evaluation does not support an ADHD diagnosis, that is still useful information. Your clinician should explain differentials and whether primary care, sleep evaluation, or another pathway fits better.

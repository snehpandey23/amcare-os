ADHD Evaluation Cost: What You’ll Pay (and Why It Varies)

Cost depends on where you go and what you get:

  • Quick online “assessments”: $50–$150. These are often questionnaires, not full clinical evaluations. Useful for screening, but not a diagnosis.
  • Traditional psychiatry (in-person): $300–$800+ per visit. May require multiple visits and long wait times.
  • Telehealth with licensed providers: $150–$350 for a comprehensive evaluation. Often faster and more transparent.

Insurance vs. Cash Pay: Which Makes Sense?

Some insurance plans cover ADHD evaluations, but you may face deductibles, copays, and prior-authorization delays. Cash pay gives you clarity—you know exactly what you’re paying, and you can often get an appointment within days instead of months.

How ADHD Evaluations Work with Licensed Providers

Your evaluation is conducted by a board-certified provider using the same clinical standards as in-person care. They’ll review your history, use validated tools, and rule out other conditions. The diagnosis and documentation are legitimate and accepted for prescriptions and school/work accommodations.

FAQ

Is $199 for an ADHD evaluation a good price?

Yes. It’s on the lower end for a comprehensive 60–90 minute evaluation with a licensed provider. Many traditional clinics charge $400–$800 or more.

Does the $199 include the prescription?

The evaluation fee covers the diagnostic visit. If medication is prescribed, you’ll pay for the medication (often through your pharmacy or a prescription plan). Ongoing medication management is $149/month if you enroll.

Can I use my FSA or HSA?

Many patients use FSA/HSA funds for ADHD evaluation and treatment. Check with your plan administrator.

What if I don’t get diagnosed with ADHD?

You still get answers. Your provider will discuss what might be going on and recommend next steps—whether that’s ruling out other conditions or exploring different supports.

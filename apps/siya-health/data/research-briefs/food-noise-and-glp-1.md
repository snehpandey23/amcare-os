## 1. Literature review summary

### Landmark & pivotal trials (weight / GLP-1)

| Study | Citation | Relevance |
|-------|----------|-----------|
| **STEP 1** | Wilding et al., *NEJM* 2021; PMID 33567185 | Semaglutide 2.4 mg + lifestyle → ~14.9% mean weight loss at 68 wk; validates GLP-1 RA efficacy; Control of Eating outcomes in program |
| **SURMOUNT program** | Tirzepatide obesity trials (2022–2024) | Dual GIP/GLP-1; comparable/better weight outcomes; relevant for “Mounjaro/Zepbound” patient questions |
| **Exenatide fMRI** | van Bloemendaal et al. (cited in Cureus 2025 narrative) | GLP-1 ↓ anticipatory reward (“wanting”), normalized consummatory (“liking”) — mechanistic basis for reduced cue reactivity |

### Systematic / narrative reviews (last 5 years)

| Source | Focus |
|--------|--------|
| Rabenda et al., narrative review on “food noise” + GLP-1 (JEHS 2025) | DMN hyperactivity, dopaminergic reward dysregulation; incretin therapies “silence” intrusive food cognition |
| Cook G., Cureus 2025 (PMC12770913) | DMN + reward circuits; mindfulness parallel; exenatide/semaglutide patient reports |
| **Changes in Eating Behaviour During Treatment With Obesity Medications** (PMC12717437, 2025) | Qualitative: “food noise” reduction as psychological relief; some return in stable phase |
| Wang et al., *Front Endocrinol* 2023 | GLP-1 RAs in obesity — mechanisms review |
| StatPearls: Semaglutide (NBK603723, updated 2025) | CNS GLP-1R → appetite, cravings, satiety |

### High-impact mechanistic studies (2024–2025)

| Study | Citation | Key finding |
|-------|----------|-------------|
| **Hedonic eating vs GLP-1R satiety** | Zhu et al., *Science* 2025; PMID 40146831 | VTA dopamine neurons sustain palatable intake; semaglutide suppresses during consumption; **compensatory rebound** possible with chronic use — hedonic drive can partially return |
| **Semaglutide + dopamine** | Kooij et al., *Neuroscience Applied* 2024 | ↓ appetite/reward-seeking; complex VTA signaling during reward collection |
| **Tirzepatide + food preoccupation** | *Nature Medicine* 2025 case series | NAc delta–theta biomarker; breakthrough preoccupation despite tirzepatide in one participant — individual variability |

### Society guidelines

| Guideline | Year | Relevance |
|-----------|------|-----------|
| **AACE ABCD Algorithm** | 2025 (Endocrine Practice; PMID 40956256) | Obesity as chronic disease; semaglutide/tirzepatide preferred for many complications; **≥5% loss at 3 months** as response benchmark; complication-centric care (not BMI-only) |
| **FDA labeling** | Wegovy, Zepbound, etc. | GI effects, thyroid C-cell rodent data, pregnancy contraindications — must surface in patient education |

### Patient-reported “food noise” data (2025)

| Source | Finding |
|--------|---------|
| Kvist et al., **EASD 2025** poster (INFORM survey, semaglutide users) | Self-reported food noise ↓ after starting semaglutide; ↓ “constant thoughts about food” (62% → 16%); parallel improvements in confidence/mental health (survey, industry-associated — interpret as hypothesis-generating) |
| Healthline / FNQ studies (2025) | Food Noise Questionnaire (5 items, 0–20); GLP-1 + lifestyle program > lifestyle alone for FNQ reduction |

**Evidence grading for article:** Mechanisms = moderate (human imaging + animal causal work). “Food noise” as formal diagnosis = **low** (lay + emerging research term). Clinical benefit on cravings = **moderate-strong** via STEP/SURMOUNT + eating-behavior qualitative data.

## 2. Audience research

### A. What patients are actually asking

- “What is food noise?”
- “Will semaglutide/Mounjaro make food noise go away?”
- “Why do I still think about food on GLP-1?”
- “Is food noise the same as hunger?”
- “I feel empty/grief when food thoughts stop — is that normal?”
- “Does food noise come back after weight loss?”
- “Can I get this effect without medication?”
- “Is this just willpower / laziness?”

### B. Common misconceptions

| Myth | Reality |
|------|---------|
| Food noise = hunger | Cognitive preoccupation can occur when physically full |
| GLP-1 “cures” emotional eating | Reward/emotion pathways may persist; therapy still matters |
| Silence on day 3 = full dose effect | Effect often strengthens with titration |
| No food noise = no need for lifestyle | Medication adjunct; protein, movement, sleep still matter |
| All GLP-1 users get quiet brain | Heterogeneous response; non-responders exist |
| Food noise reduction proves counterfeit product is “working” | Dangerous inference; only regulated care + monitoring |

### C. What physicians commonly overlook

- **Psychological adaptation** to decades of food-centric cognition (identity, social life, coping)
- **ADHD / impulsivity / binge eating** overlap — GLP-1 alone may not suffice
- **Return of food noise** in maintenance or after weight loss (Science 2025 compensatory mechanisms)
- **Muscle/protein** when appetite drops abruptly
- **Eating disorder history** — screen before intensifying pharmacotherapy
- **Alcohol/nicotine** reward pathway shifts (patients report, limited RCT data)

### D. Evidence changed (last 3–5 years)

- STEP/SURMOUNT normalized **10–15%+** pharmacologic weight loss
- “Food noise” entered **patient lexicon** tied to GLP-1 (2023–2025)
- Mechanistic shift: **central reward** not just gastric emptying
- 2025 **VTA dopamine opposition** paper complicates “permanent quiet” narrative
- AACE 2025: **complication-centric** obesity care vs weight-only

### E. Inaccurate online advice

- Compounded “same as Wegovy” without medical supervision
- Promising permanent elimination of all cravings
- Using food noise silence to justify **under-eating / skipping protein**
- Ignoring GI red flags to stay on dose
- Equating non-response with “fake” drug only (dose, adherence, ED, sleep matter)

### Top patient frustrations (synthesis)

1. Years of failed diets framed as moral failure  
2. Disorientation when intrusive food thoughts stop  
3. Nausea vs desired appetite suppression tradeoff  
4. Cost, insurance, shortage, compound confusion  
5. Fear weight and “noise” return if medication stops  

### Top Reddit / forum themes

- “Brain finally quiet” / relief narratives  
- Grief, boredom, “forgetting to eat”  
- Dose titration timeline (“louder at 0.25 mg”)  
- Still craving specific foods (pizza, alcohol)

## 3. Controversies (cover honestly)

1. **Is “food noise” a valid clinical construct?** — Useful patient language; not DSM diagnosis.  
2. **Industry-linked survey data (EASD 2025)** — Informative but not independent RCT endpoint.  
3. **Durability of cognitive suppression** — Qualitative data suggest partial return for some.  
4. **Mouse VTA data → humans** — Plausible but not fully mapped in humans.  
5. **Compounded GLP-1** — Regulatory and safety debate (address without endorsing gray market).

---

## 4. Clinical pearls (for article)

- Distinguish **homeostatic hunger**, **hedonic craving**, **food noise**, **emotional eating**.  
- Titrate slowly; GI tolerance predicts adherence.  
- Screen **BED**, bulimia history, pregnancy intent.  
- Pair pharmacotherapy with **protein-forward** nutrition and resistance training.  
- Plan **maintenance** early (behavior + medication strategy per AACE).  
- If ADHD drives impulsive eating, coordinate **ADHD + metabolic** care.

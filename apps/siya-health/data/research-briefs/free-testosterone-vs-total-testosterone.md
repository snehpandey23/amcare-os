# Research Brief: Free Testosterone vs Total Testosterone

**Title:** Free Testosterone vs Total Testosterone: What Patients Should Know  
**Strategic role:** Hormone health authority; answers “normal testosterone but I still feel terrible”  
**Audience:** Educated patients  
**Date:** June 2026

---

## Core frustration

> “My doctor said my testosterone is normal. Why do I still have low libido, fatigue, and brain fog?”

**Answer in one line:** Total testosterone measures everything in the blood; only a small unbound fraction (free T) and loosely albumin-bound fraction are bioavailable—and SHBG determines how much is locked up.

---

## Guideline anchors

| Source | Key points |
|--------|------------|
| **Endocrine Society 2018** | Diagnose hypogonadism only with symptoms + unequivocally low T; morning fasting total T; repeat; if borderline or altered SHBG → free T by equilibrium dialysis or accurate formula; avoid direct analog free T assays |
| **AUA 2018 (reaffirmed 2024)** | Total T &lt;300 ng/dL reasonable cut-off with symptoms; two separate morning samples; free/bioavailable T when binding proteins altered; on TRT with low-normal total T → consider SHBG + reliable free T |
| **Rosner et al. Endocrine Society position** | Assay limitations; free T measurement pitfalls |
| **CDC/AUA harmonization** | Assay CV variability; need standardized reference ranges |

## Definitions

- **Total testosterone (TT):** All circulating T (SHBG-bound + albumin-bound + free).
- **Free testosterone (FT):** ~2–3% unbound; enters cells to exert androgen effects.
- **Bioavailable testosterone:** Free + albumin-bound (dissociates readily).
- **SHBG:** Liver protein; high SHBG → less free T at same total; low SHBG → more free T at same total.
- **Albumin-bound:** ~30–50% loosely bound.

**Calculated FT:** Vermeulen/Sodergard equations using TT, SHBG, albumin—preferred over direct RIA analog assays (often ~7× discrepancy vs equilibrium dialysis).

## Obesity paradox (landmark data)

- **PMID 25777143:** Obesity lowers SHBG more than aging raises it; obese men lower total T but **calculated free T often similar** to non-obese → risk of over-diagnosing hypogonadism from total T alone.
- **Longitudinal obese cohort:** Majority with low TT + low SHBG had **normal FT** without hypogonadal symptoms; low TT + low FT subset more likely symptoms—FT prevents overtreatment.

---

## Sleep apnea

- OSA associated with lower total T; **CPAP meta-analyses:** no consistent significant rise in total or free T (Front Endocrinol 2019; PLOS One).
- **Endocrine Society:** Untreated severe OSA → avoid starting TRT.
- Treat apnea for sleep/cardiovascular reasons; do not expect CPAP alone to “fix” hormones.

---

## Symptoms vs labs mismatch — causes

1. High SHBG → normal total, low free  
2. Low SHBG → low total, adequate free  
3. Non-hormonal: OSA, depression, ADHD, thyroid, iron, sleep debt  
4. Wrong sample (afternoon, non-fasting, acute illness)  
5. Assay/lab variability; wrong reference range  
6. Anabolic steroid / opiate suppression of axis  
7. Secondary hypogonadism (low LH) — prolactinoma, meds

## B. Misconceptions

| Myth | Fact |
|------|------|
| Normal total = no problem | SHBG can hide low free |
| Free T on lab slip is always accurate | Direct immunoassays often wrong; ask method |
| Low total always needs TRT | Obese men may have low total, normal free |
| High total = optimal | Very high SHBG → symptomatic with mid total |
| TRT fixes fatigue without diagnosis | Treat reversible causes first |
| Every man 40+ needs TRT | Guidelines against routine TRT by age alone |
| CPAP raises testosterone reliably | Meta-analyses negative |

---

## C. Lab interpretation mistakes

- Single afternoon sample  
- No repeat confirmation  
- Ignoring SHBG when total borderline  
- Using FAI alone (deprecated in some contexts when SHBG &lt;30)  
- Comparing results across different labs/assays  
- Starting TRT without LH/FSH, prolactin when indicated  

---

## D. TRT clinic oversimplification

- Symptom questionnaires → immediate gel  
- No fertility discussion  
- No hematocrit/PSA baseline  
- “Optimal” 800–1200 ng/dL marketing  
- Bioidentical pellet bundles  
- No sleep apnea screen  
- Free T from unreliable assay labeled “normal”

## Recent evidence (≤5 years)

- **TRAVERSE (NEJM 2023):** TRT noninferior to placebo for MACE in symptomatic men with CVD risk + T&lt;300; higher atrial fibrillation, pulmonary embolism, AKI on TRT  
- **SHBG/obesity interpretation (ongoing relevance)**  
- **LC-MS/MS free T methods (2025)** — calculated vs equilibrium dialysis in PCOS populations; method matters

Health guide

Perimenopause and menopause

If you are in your 40s or 50s, your cycles are changing, and you are trying to sort whether brain fog, sleep breaks, or hot flashes are “just aging,” hormones, ADHD, or something else—this guide is for you.

Educational only: General education, not personal medical advice, diagnosis, or a treatment plan for you. A licensed clinician decides what applies to your situation.

Clinician-informed

Written for patients from menopause society guidance—not a substitute for a visit.

What this page covers—and what it does not

  • Covers: perimenopause vs menopause; mood and focus in the transition; keeping hot-flash treatment claims separate from cognition/dementia claims.
  • Does not cover: personal menopause staging; dosing; or a promise of hormone therapy.

What this midlife pattern is

The clinical job is to name the transition accurately, separate hot-flash treatment from cognition claims, and keep ADHD history in view without inventing a hormone-only explanation.

Perimenopause vs menopause—said plainly

  • Perimenopause — the transition years before menopause, when cycles often become irregular and symptoms like hot flashes, night sweats, sleep disruption, or mood change can appear.
  • Menopause — the point marked after you have gone 12 months in a row without a period (when that pattern is not explained by another cause such as pregnancy, surgery, or certain medicines). 2

Perimenopause is the path. Menopause is the landmark after a full year without bleeding.

Mood, focus, and what often travels with the transition

Many midlife women notice changes in memory, attention, or concentration during the menopause transition. An International Menopause Society white paper says those cognitive complaints should not be confused with dementia, that dementia in midlife is rare, and that most women who go through menopause will not develop dementia. 1

Depression, sleep problems, and hot flashes/night sweats in perimenopause are associated with cognitive complaints. 1 That is a reason to ask about sleep and mood—see the sleep guide and mental health and ADHD guide—not a shortcut to a hormone prescription.

Adult ADHD remains a long-pattern diagnosis (several symptoms before age 12, impairment in more than one setting, not better explained by another condition). 3 A change around a cycle or around menopause does not create that history, and it does not erase it. Females are more likely than males to present mainly with inattentive features, which is why the history has to be taken carefully. 3 This page does not claim that month-to-month hormone swings reliably change ADHD scores.

Hormone therapy: two different claims (keep them separate)

  1. Hot flashes and night sweats: Hormone therapy can be an effective treatment for bothersome vasomotor symptoms, with its own individualized risk discussion. This page is not a prescribing guide for that decision. 2
  2. Cognition and dementia prevention: Hormone therapy is not recommended to treat cognitive complaints or to prevent dementia. 2

Iron deficiency and a sleep disorder can look like the same midlife story—see exhaustion and sleep. A headache that tracks the cycle, or a new headache in these years, belongs in a visit conversation on the headache page.

What a visit involves here: bleeding pattern, nights, mood, medicines, and which symptom is actually limiting work. A hormone discussion, when it happens, follows that history. It is an evaluation—not a promise of a prescription.

Honest limits for this section: definitions, evaluation framing, and what major position statements say about hormone therapy indications—not personal menopause staging and not a treatment plan.

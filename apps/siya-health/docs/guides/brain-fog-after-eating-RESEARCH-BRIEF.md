## Evidence summary

### Physiology (non-pathologic)

- Postprandial redirect of blood flow and parasympathetic activation after meals can increase subjective sleepiness, especially with large lunches (cardiovascular/meal-timing reviews, e.g. O’Keefe et al., JACC 2014, PMID 25225201).
- “Post-lunch dip” is partly circadian (early-afternoon alertness nadir) plus meal size—not always metabolic disease.

### Metabolic / glycemic

- **Insulin resistance** and compensatory hyperinsulinemia can precede abnormal A1C; ADA Standards of Care (2025) emphasize prediabetes screening and lifestyle-first intervention in high-risk adults.
- **Diabetes Prevention Program** (NEJM 2002, PMID 12023865): ~5–7% weight loss and activity materially reduced diabetes incidence in prediabetic adults—relevant to insulin sensitivity messaging.
- **Glycemic variability** associations with cognitive symptoms appear in systematic-review literature (heterogeneous endpoints; educate without overclaiming causality).
- **Reactive hypoglycemia** as a patient label often reflects symptoms 1–4h post meal; diagnosis requires clinician interpretation of mixed meals, glucose monitoring, or exclusion of other causes—not internet thresholds.

### Sleep & fatigue overlap

- Sleep restriction impairs cognition (Killgore review; Van Dongen sleep debt studies).
- Untreated **OSA** amplifies daytime sleepiness; post-meal timing can feel like “crash on top of debt.”

### Hormonal / stress

- Cortisol dysregulation from chronic stress affects meal choice and perceived energy; not a substitute for glucose testing when metabolic red flags exist.

### AACE / obesity medicine framing

- Cardiometabolic risk clusters (visceral adiposity, BP, lipids, glycemia, cravings) — post-meal symptoms fit history-taking, not standalone diagnosis.

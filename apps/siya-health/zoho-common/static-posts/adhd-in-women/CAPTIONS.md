## Instagram caption

ADHD in women often looks quiet.

Not the stereotypical hyperactivity — more often daydreaming, disorganization, emotional intensity, and a lifelong sense of “why is everything harder for me?”

Hormonal shifts, social expectations, and misattribution to anxiety or mood can delay recognition for years. Rejection sensitivity and internalized shame are common.

Screening is not diagnosis. A structured evaluation with a licensed clinician explores childhood history, cross-setting patterns, and look-alikes — not an online quiz alone.

## LinkedIn caption

ADHD in women is frequently under-recognized because it often presents as inattention, overwhelm, and emotional dysregulation — not obvious hyperactivity.

Clinical and research themes that matter in patient education:
- Inattentive patterns can be mistaken for anxiety, burnout, or “just stress”
- Hormonal transitions may change how symptoms show up
- Social masking and high expectations can hide impairment until adulthood
- Evaluation should still seek childhood developmental history, even when records are sparse

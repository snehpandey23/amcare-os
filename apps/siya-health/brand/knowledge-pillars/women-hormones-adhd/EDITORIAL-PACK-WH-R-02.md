## Insight

Lifelong coping that worked for decades can collapse in the 40s when sleep, iron, mood, hormones, and attention patterns move together — and medicine often treats them as separate appointments.

## Evidence / claim ladder

- SAFE: Many women notice midlife cognitive / coping changes; late ADHD recognition is common; systems interact.
- CAUTION: Cycle-aware med ideas — clinician only (not in founder posts).

### Draft A (canonical — use this)

I've been wondering why so many women in their 40s tell me the same story in different words.

"I used to hold everything in my head."
"Now I walk into a room and forget why I'm there."
"My coping used to work."

We call it brain fog and file it under perimenopause—which is often fair.

But medicine rarely asks the next question:

What if sleep, iron, mood, hormones, and lifelong attention patterns are all moving at once—
and we keep treating them like separate appointments?

I still don't fully understand why our systems are so good at splitting the human into departments
and so bad at owning the whole person.

Maybe I'm wrong.
Maybe specialization is the only way.

Or maybe midlife is just when the seams finally show.

### Draft B (reserve / shorter)

Medicine taught me to name a symptom.

It didn't always teach me who owns the whole human when the symptom is actually a system.

A woman in her 40s with fog, wrecked sleep, and coping that suddenly fails
isn't looking for a blog category.

She's looking for someone willing to sit with the mess without forcing one label too early.

I've been wondering what healthcare would look like if we designed for that.

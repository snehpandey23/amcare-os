## 1. Evidence strength (do not overclaim)

| Claim type | Status | Safe public language |
|------------|--------|----------------------|
| Hormones “cause” ADHD | **False / unsupported** | Never use |
| Estrogen–dopamine interaction is biologically plausible | Supported by neurobiology reviews | “Estrogen interacts with dopamine pathways involved in attention” |
| Many women report worse ADHD before period / luteal phase | Suggestive; small studies + clinical observation | “Many women report…” / “associated with…” |
| Sex hormones ↔ ADHD symptoms (systematic review, 2025) | **11 studies**, small samples, heterogeneous | “Emerging evidence suggests association; evidence base is limited” |
| Premenstrual stimulant dose increase | **Case series (n=9)** de Jong 2023 — promising, not guideline | “Some clinicians explore… under supervision after tracking” — **never DIY dosing advice** |
| Perimenopause worsens ADHD / unmasks it | Strong clinical consensus + reviews; RCTs thin | “Common clinical pattern; needs individualized evaluation” |
| HRT as ADHD treatment | Speculative / adjunct in specialist practice | Do **not** market HRT as ADHD treatment |

**Rule for Siya copy:** association + clinical pattern + uncertainty. No “hormones fix ADHD.” No “proven cycle dosing protocol.”

## 2. Key papers (foundation set)

1. **Osianlis et al., 2025** — *ADHD and Sex Hormones in Females: A Systematic Review* (J Atten Disord).  
   [PMC12145478](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12145478/)  
   - 11 studies; suggestive link at puberty + menstrual cycle; menopause understudied.  
   - Limitation: tiny evidence base.

2. **de Jong et al., 2023** — Premenstrual psychostimulant dose adjustment (case series, n=9).  
   [PMC10751335](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10751335/)  
   - +30–50% premenstrual dose in selected patients; improved ADHD/mood reports.  
   - **Not** a protocol to publish as standard of care.

3. **Female ADHD / lifelong hormones review, 2025** — Frontiers/PMC12277363.  
   - Puberty → cycle → pregnancy → peri/menopause interplay with mood/cognition.  
   - Survey-style self-report data in places — use carefully.

4. **Older experimental** — d-amphetamine subjective effects vary by cycle phase (follicular > luteal in some studies). Supports “meds may feel different,” not automatic dose change.

## 3. Decision points for you

### A. Medication timing / luteal dosing
**Recommendation:** Educate that *some women* notice reduced stimulant effect premenstrually; invite cycle tracking; say dose changes require clinician judgment.  
**Avoid:** “Increase your dose before your period.”

**Approve?** ☐ Educate-only · ☐ Mention case-series cautiously · ☐ Omit dosing entirely for v1

### B. PMDD
High overlap in patient stories; high misdiagnosis risk.  
**Recommendation:** Separate section: “ADHD + PMDD can co-occur; PMDD is not ADHD; evaluation should distinguish mood cyclicity from lifelong executive patterns.”

**Approve?** ☐ Include PMDD differential · ☐ FAQ-only · ☐ Defer spoke

### C. Pregnancy / breastfeeding
High regulatory + liability surface for telehealth ADHD.  
**Recommendation:** Short, conservative section + “discuss with OB + ADHD clinician”; no stimulant marketing in pregnancy content.

**Approve?** ☐ Short conservative · ☐ Full spoke later · ☐ Link-out only

**Approve?** ☐ Include · ☐ Minimal · ☐ Defer

# Facebook Caption — AD-R-02 (The Child Mirror)

Many adults receive their first ADHD diagnosis not because they sought one for themselves, but because they recognized their own story while helping their child through testing.

As you check the boxes—trouble getting started on tasks, intense emotional reactions, chronic disorganization despite intense effort, misplacing essentials daily—something clicks:

"This isn't just my child. This has been me my entire life."

ADHD has a strong genetic component. Generations of adults grew up without an evaluation because their symptoms were internalized, masked by good grades, or written off as personality flaws.

A late diagnosis isn't about looking back with regret—it's about finally understanding how your brain works and accessing real support.

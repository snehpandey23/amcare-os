# LinkedIn Company Caption — AD-R-02 (The Child Mirror)

A significant percentage of adults diagnosed with ADHD in their 30s, 40s, and 50s share a common inflection point: advocating for their children.

During the developmental history and behavioral screening process for a child, parents frequently encounter diagnostic criteria that mirror their own lifelong struggles:
• Executive dysfunction hidden behind overworking
• Working memory challenges compensated for by elaborate coping mechanisms
• Emotional regulation strain internalized as personal deficiency

Because historical diagnostic frameworks focused primarily on disruptive classroom behaviors in boys, high-masking girls and inattentive presentations were frequently overlooked.

Recognizing these patterns in adulthood provides clinical clarity, opening the door to evidence-based interventions, workplace accommodations, and structured care pathways.

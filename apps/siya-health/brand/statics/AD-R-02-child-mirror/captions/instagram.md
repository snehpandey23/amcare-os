For many adults, the moment of ADHD recognition doesn’t happen in a doctor’s office for themselves. 

It happens at the kitchen table, filling out an evaluation packet for their child.

Reading questions about time blindness, task initiation, emotional sensitivity, and losing track of thoughts mid-sentence—and realizing you aren’t just describing your 8-year-old. You’re describing your entire life.

ADHD is highly heritable. Many parents who grew up masked, high-achieving, or simply labeled “scatterbrained” only discover their own neurodivergence when advocating for their kids.

You weren’t broken, lazy, or failing. You were managing a brain that was never given the right manual.

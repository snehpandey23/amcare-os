## Opening

If you’re pregnant — or planning to be — and you take ADHD medication, the internet often offers two extremes: *stop everything* or *don’t worry about it*.

A 2025 systematic review of the last five years of research suggests a more careful middle path: for many prescribed ADHD medications, recent high-quality observational studies often do **not** show a clear, large harm signal for mother or baby — while untreated ADHD and abrupt decisions made without clinical support also carry real risks. One medicine, **modafinil**, stands out with a clearer warning signal and needs a different conversation.

---

## Why this question is hard

ADHD usually begins long before pregnancy and often continues through it. Pregnancy and early parenting can make organization, sleep, and stress harder — which is exactly when some people feel they need their usual supports most.

At the same time, pregnancy safety data for ADHD medicines has historically been limited, and many clinicians were trained to advise stopping medication by default. Research has been catching up: more studies now try to separate the effects of **having ADHD** from the effects of **treating ADHD**.

---

## What the 2025 review looked at

Researchers reviewed **12 cohort studies** published roughly mid-2019 to mid-2024. All scored as high quality on a standard observational checklist. Across studies, millions of pregnancy records were examined (individual studies ranged from small clinic samples to multi-million registry analyses).

They looked at prescribed ADHD medicines (including common stimulants and several non-stimulants) and a wide range of maternal and infant outcomes. Because studies used different medicines, timing, and endpoints, the authors used a **narrative synthesis** — not a single pooled “yes/no” meta-analysis.

**Important limits:** Most registry studies know that a prescription was filled, not that every dose was taken. Dose and duration were generally not studied. Observational data can still leave residual confounding.

---

## What newer evidence suggests (without overclaiming)

Across the included studies:

- **Several** found no significant increase in many adverse outcomes when ADHD medication continued in pregnancy.
- Some work suggests **benefits of continuing** treatment for people who need it — or risks when medication is stopped — though individual findings need care (one smaller study linked stopping dexamfetamine with higher odds of threatened abortion; that signal was not the main story of larger datasets).
- A few studies still reported associations with outcomes such as certain birth defects or pre-eclampsia; after better adjustment, many associations **attenuated**, and authors often pointed to confounding by indication (ADHD and related health factors) rather than a simple drug effect.

**Plain language:** The scientific conversation is moving away from automatic “always stop” folklore toward **individualized shared decisions** — still cautious, still incomplete.

---

## The modafinil exception

The review highlights **modafinil** differently: first-trimester exposure was associated with a significantly higher risk of major congenital malformations in the cited analyses.

**Content rule for Siya:** Treat modafinil as a **drug-specific caution** that belongs in a clinician conversation — not as proof that “all ADHD meds are dangerous in pregnancy,” and not as DIY stop/start advice in a caption.

---

## What this means for patients (education, not instructions)

1. **Don’t decide from TikTok or a single headline.**  
2. **Bring your full picture** to your clinicians: which medicine, dose, how well it works, other conditions, and pregnancy timing.  
3. **Ask about your specific medicine** — especially if it is modafinil or if you’ve been told conflicting advice.  
4. **Ask about untreated ADHD risks too** — mood, functioning, safety behaviors, and postpartum mental health can matter for mother and baby.  
5. **Expect shared decision-making** between psychiatry/ADHD care and obstetric care — not a one-line rule.

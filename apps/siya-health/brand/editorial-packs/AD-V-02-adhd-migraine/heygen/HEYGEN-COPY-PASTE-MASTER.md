## V2 — Aura / visual disturbance (data)

```
The ADHD–migraine link is a little stronger when migraine comes with visual aura.

---

Aura can look like flashes, zig-zags, or temporary visual changes before the pain. Research in adults found migraine and ADHD symptoms co-occur more often than chance — especially with those visual disturbances.

---

That is not a diagnosis from a video. It’s a reason to tell your clinician the full story: attention patterns and migraine features, not just “bad headaches.”

---

If this resonates, track aura days separately for two weeks, then ask whether both deserve a real workup. Educational only — not medical advice.

```

For one week, mark days as overloaded, understimulated, or okay — next to migraine yes or no. Bring it to a clinician. Educational content only — not medical advice.

## V5 — Stimulants (clinical stamp preferred)

```
ADHD meds and migraines: both stories are real.

---

Some adults say stimulants were one of the most helpful changes for their head pain. Others say the same meds triggered migraines — sometimes through appetite loss, sleep disruption, or skipped meals.

---

Migraines also show up in people with ADHD who never take stimulants. So this isn’t a simple “meds good” or “meds bad” reel.

---

If you’re on ADHD medication and tracking headaches, bring timing, meals, sleep, and migraine days to your prescribing clinician. Do not start, stop, or change doses from social media. Educational only — not medical advice.

## V8 — Hemiplegic migraine (clinical stamp required)

```
Someone asked about hemiplegic migraine. Here’s the careful version.

---

Hemiplegic migraine is a rare migraine subtype that can include temporary weakness on one side of the body — and it can look frighteningly like a stroke.

---

Social media cannot tell you which one you have. Sudden weakness, facial droop, trouble speaking, or new neurological symptoms need urgent medical evaluation — same day, emergency pathways when in doubt.

---

If you’ve been labeled with complex or hemiplegic migraine, that care belongs with specialists who know your history — not a Reel. This is educational only — not medical advice, and not a diagnosis.

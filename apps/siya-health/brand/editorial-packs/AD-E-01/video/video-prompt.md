# AD-E-01 — HeyGen video script (avatar-free, evaluation-focused)

**Insight ID:** AD-E-01  
**Topic:** ADHD & emotional dysregulation — "nothing halfway"  
**Format:** Short-form reel · 9:16 · **~50–55 seconds**  
**Mode:** **NO AVATAR** — voiceover + branded, script-matched background scenes  
**Tone:** Professional · validating · non-shaming · hopeful  
**Audience:** Adults 18–55 — overload/freeze, self-blame, work–relationship strain, fear of stigma  
**Funnel:** Awareness → consideration → **evaluation** (this video's objective is to move people to take the free screening, not just to inform)  
**Structure:** Validate the lived pattern → correct the willpower myth → name it as an overlooked ADHD pattern → brief clinical anchor → **ends on an open question** → **Siya Health answers** → evaluation CTA

**Clinical anchor (brief, on-script):** Research on adult ADHD links emotional dysregulation to real difficulty in daily functioning and mental health — described as a pattern, not a standalone diagnosis on its own.  
**Do not claim:** Medication/stimulant effects, bipolar misdiagnosis statistics, RSD as a formal diagnosis, ADHD-vs-PTSD comparison, or any invented statistic. No self-diagnosis language.

**Disclaimer (closing card):** Educational. Not medical advice or a diagnosis. If you're in crisis, contact local emergency services or a crisis line.

Nothing halfway.
With ADHD, feelings don't always arrive at a normal volume.

One moment you're completely overloaded. The next, you're frozen — while everyone else has already moved on.

That's not a lack of willpower.
It's emotional dysregulation — feelings that are hard to steer, not feelings you're choosing to have.

It's one of the most overlooked sides of ADHD — not just focus, not just fidgeting.
Research on adult ADHD links this kind of emotional intensity to real difficulty at work and in relationships.

One thing that helps: track it for two weeks — what triggered it, how long it lasted, how much sleep you got.

So is this just a rough patch — or something your brain has been carrying for a long time?

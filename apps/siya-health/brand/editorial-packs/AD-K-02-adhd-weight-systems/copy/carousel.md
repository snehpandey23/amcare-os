# Phase 1 Copy Plan — AD-K-02 (Build for Real Life)

**Status:** Phase 1 review only
**Anchor submitted:** https://siya.health/blog/combining-adhd-treatment-and-weight-loss-strategies
**Current canonical:** https://siya.health/answers/adhd-and-weight-loss-connection
**Intent:** Knowledge / recognition
**Template:** A-03 lean
**Slide count:** 5
**One realization:** A workable plan reduces decisions and fits low-capacity days; ADHD and weight goals can be discussed together without turning either treatment into a shortcut.

**Excluded:** statistics, obesity-causation language, drug names, medication comparisons, efficacy/safety claims, before/after imagery, testimonial outcomes.

- 4:5, 1080×1350
- Cream `#F4EFE7`

- Soft cream dissolve on left into one warm, full-opacity photo on right

## Slide 1 — Hook

**Headline:** Your plan needs **fewer decisions**

**Recognition:** ADHD-friendly weight support starts with the life you actually live.

**Accent:** fewer decisions

**Visual guidelines:**

- Warm morning kitchen; adult looking at a simple breakfast choice
- Calm recognition, not distress or “failed diet” body language
- One or two realistic food items, not an aspirational meal-prep spread

**Claim risk:** MEDIUM — general ADHD/weight support framing; no mechanism or outcome claim.

---

## Slide 2 — Real-life planning

**Headline:** Stop planning for **fantasy you**

**Recognition:** Choose meals your tired, distracted self can still make.

**Accent:** fantasy you

**Visual guidelines:**

- Adult opening a refrigerator with a few visible, ordinary options
- Everyday clothing and lived-in but uncluttered setting
- Avoid “perfect wellness kitchen” styling

**Claim risk:** LOW — practical recognition language.

---

## Slide 3 — Defaults

**Headline:** Give meals **a default**

**Recognition:** A few familiar options can make “what now?” one less daily battle.

**Accent:** a default

**Visual guidelines:**

- Two or three repeatable meal components visible in clear containers
- Subject choosing one option without a recipe, tracker, or calorie display
- Photo implies reduced choice; no checklist on-frame

**Claim risk:** LOW — optional practical support, not a weight-loss outcome claim.

---

## Slide 4 — Backup plan

**Headline:** Keep a **no-prep backup**

**Recognition:** A realistic fallback is still a plan—not a failure.

**Accent:** no-prep backup

**Visual guidelines:**

- Grab-and-go food placed at eye level or carried to a desk
- Low-capacity-day realism without messy-chaos stereotypes
- No branded supplement, shake, medication, or diet product

**Claim risk:** LOW — validation and practical option.

---

## Slide 5 — Coordinated close

**Headline:** Put **both goals** together

**Recognition:** ADHD treatment and weight goals belong in the same clinician conversation.

**Accent:** both goals

**Visual guidelines:**

- Calm adult preparing notes before a telehealth visit or seated in conversation
- Notebook may show neutral headings such as “focus,” “appetite,” and “routine”; no numbers or prescriptions
- No CTA button; the compositor footer remains the only footer treatment

**Claim risk:** MEDIUM — coordination language, not individualized treatment advice.

# Facebook Caption — AD-K-01 (The Bandwidth Cliff)

Why are so many capable adults receiving their first ADHD diagnosis in their 30s, 40s, and 50s?

Because they didn’t suddenly develop ADHD—their coping strategies simply reached a breaking point.

In childhood and early adulthood, strict school routines and external pressure provided the structure their brain couldn’t generate on its own. Many high-achieving adults spent years overcompensating, staying up late to finish assignments, and running on last-minute adrenaline.

Then adulthood brought new, unstructured demands:
- Remote work without clear guardrails
- The compounding mental load of parenthood
- Career promotions requiring complex project management
- Hormonal transitions in midlife

When the scaffolding fell away, the executive strain became undeniable.

An adult ADHD evaluation isn't about looking back with regret—it's about gaining clarity, ruling out overlapping conditions, and accessing support that actually fits your brain.

## 2. Facebook
Why are so many capable adults receiving their first ADHD diagnosis in their 30s, 40s, and 50s?

Because they didn’t suddenly develop ADHD—their coping strategies simply reached a breaking point.

In childhood and early adulthood, strict school routines and external pressure provided the structure their brain couldn’t generate on its own. Many high-achieving adults spent years overcompensating, staying up late to finish assignments, and running on last-minute adrenaline.

Then adulthood brought new, unstructured demands:
- Remote work without clear guardrails
- The compounding mental load of parenthood
- Career promotions requiring complex project management
- Hormonal transitions in midlife

When the scaffolding fell away, the executive strain became undeniable.

An adult ADHD evaluation isn't about looking back with regret—it's about gaining clarity, ruling out overlapping conditions, and accessing support that actually fits your brain.

## 3. LinkedIn Company
A common clinical pattern in adult psychiatry is the high-performing professional who presents with sudden cognitive overwhelm in their late 30s or 40s.

Often misattributed to standalone burnout or general anxiety, these inflection points frequently reveal undiagnosed, lifelong ADHD.

Why does recognition happen so late?

1. Compensatory Scaffolding: External academic structure, high intelligence, and perfectionism masked underlying executive dysfunction for years.
2. The Bandwidth Cliff: Transitions into unstructured remote environments, executive roles, or parenthood exponentially increase working memory demands.
3. Diagnostic Evolution: Earlier clinical models emphasized overt childhood hyperactivity, leaving quiet inattention and internalized symptoms unrecognized.

A comprehensive adult ADHD evaluation evaluates lifelong symptom persistence across multiple environments, distinguishes executive dysfunction from mood and sleep disorders, and establishes evidence-based care plans.

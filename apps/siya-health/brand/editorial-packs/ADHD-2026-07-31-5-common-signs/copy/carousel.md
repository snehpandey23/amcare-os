# Carousel — 5 Common Signs of Adult ADHD

Insight: Adult ADHD often shows up as lived friction — starting, time, attention, feelings, forgetfulness — not only childhood hyperactivity stereotypes.

Practical change: If several patterns are longstanding and impairing, pursue a structured clinical evaluation — social media is not a diagnosis.

## Slide 1 — Hook
5 Common Signs of Adult ADHD

## Slide 2 — Starting feels impossible
Recognition: You know exactly what needs doing — and somehow never begin.
Explanation: Getting started can take more effort than the task itself, even when the plan is clear.
Takeaway: Difficulty initiating tasks is a common adult ADHD pattern linked to executive functioning.

## Slide 3 — Time keeps slipping
Recognition: Five more minutes becomes an hour. Deadlines arrive as surprises.
Explanation: Estimating, tracking, and feeling time can be unreliable — not a simple motivation problem.
Takeaway: Time-management strain is a frequent adult presentation in clinical guidance.

## Slide 4 — Attention drifts midstream
Recognition: You're in the meeting — and suddenly you've missed the point.
Explanation: Sustaining attention can break even when you care about the outcome.
Takeaway: Inattention is a core DSM-5-TR feature of ADHD — in adults as well as children.

## Slide 5 — Feelings hit harder
Recognition: Frustration spikes fast. Recovery takes longer than you want.
Explanation: Many adults with ADHD report emotion intensity and low frustration tolerance.
Takeaway: Emotion dysregulation is common in adult ADHD research — not a required DSM criterion alone.

## Slide 6 — Details keep vanishing
Recognition: Keys, bills, messages, the thing you just put down — gone again.
Explanation: Forgetfulness in daily life can persist despite reminders and good intentions.
Takeaway: Day-to-day forgetfulness is a classic inattentive symptom in adult assessment.

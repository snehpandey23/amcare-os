## Slide 2 — The lived stack

**Slide num:** 1 of 5  
**Headline:** It’s not  
“just one thing.”  
**Recognition:** ADHD. Insulin resistance. Sometimes PCOS.  
**Explanation:** Many adults describe all three showing up together — and feeling passed between specialties.  
**Takeaway:** A stacked picture is common. It doesn’t mean any one label is fake.

---

## Slide 3 — What science supports

**Slide num:** 2 of 5  
**Headline:** Association  
≠ causation  
**Recognition:** Research links ADHD with higher metabolic risk — including insulin resistance patterns.  
**Explanation:** Shared genetics and lifestyle factors may play a role. That is not the same as “insulin resistance causes ADHD.”  
**Takeaway:** The link is real enough to take seriously — not settled enough to oversimplify.

---

## Slide 4 — Why it feels like rivalry

**Slide num:** 3 of 5  
**Headline:** Why people  
feel dismissed  
**Recognition:** “Fix the metabolism” vs “treat the ADHD” can sound like competing stories.  
**Explanation:** Focus, energy, hunger, and mood can all overlap. Treating only one lane may leave symptoms unfinished.  
**Takeaway:** Whole-person care beats either/or debates.

---

## Slide 5 — PCOS in the picture

**Slide num:** 4 of 5  
**Headline:** Where PCOS  
fits in  
**Recognition:** PCOS often travels with insulin resistance — and many women with ADHD report the overlap.  
**Explanation:** Hormones, metabolism, and attention can interact. That does not make PCOS “secret ADHD,” or ADHD “secret PCOS.”  
**Takeaway:** Name each condition clearly. Evaluate the full set.

---

## Slide 6 — What to ask

**Slide num:** 5 of 5  
**Headline:** Better questions  
to ask  
**Recognition:** You don’t need a social-media diagnosis.  
**Explanation:** Ask about attention history, metabolic labs when appropriate, sleep, cycle/PCOS history, mood, and how symptoms change together.  
**Takeaway:** Good evaluation connects the dots — it doesn’t pick a side.

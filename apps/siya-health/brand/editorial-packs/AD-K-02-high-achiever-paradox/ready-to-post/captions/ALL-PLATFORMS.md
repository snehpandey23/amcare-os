## Instagram

Good grades can hide a lot: late nights, deadline adrenaline, elaborate systems, and the energy spent looking organized.

Achievement does not confirm—or rule out—ADHD. A careful evaluation considers developmental history, impairment across settings, sleep, mood, and other explanations.

Practical next step: write down examples from school, work, relationships, and home, then bring that timeline to a qualified clinician.

Educational content only—not a diagnosis. Social media cannot replace a clinical evaluation.

## Facebook

Doing well in school does not tell the whole story. Some adults remember good grades alongside chronic procrastination, lost belongings, and needing far more structure than their peers.

Those experiences do not establish ADHD, but achievement alone should not end the conversation. A comprehensive evaluation considers childhood history, current impairment, sleep, mood, and overlapping conditions.

Educational content only—not a diagnosis.

## Slide 2 — What people mean by “physical”

**Slide num:** 1 of 5  
**Headline:** What “physical”  
usually means  
**Accent:** physical  
**Recognition:** Fidgeting. Restless legs. Trouble sitting still.  
**Explanation:**
- These are body cues people notice most.
- Adults may also feel restlessness inside.
**Takeaway:** Visible movement is only one piece of the picture.

---

## Slide 3 — Why fidgeting shows up

**Slide num:** 2 of 5  
**Headline:** Why fidgeting  
shows up  
**Accent:** fidgeting  
**Recognition:** It’s often the body trying to stay regulated.  
**Explanation:**
- Movement can help some people focus.
- It isn’t rudeness — and it isn’t proof alone.
**Takeaway:** Fidgeting can appear with ADHD. It isn’t a test.

---

## Slide 4 — You can have ADHD without looking hyper

**Slide num:** 3 of 5  
**Headline:** Not everyone  
looks hyperactive  
**Accent:** hyperactive  
**Recognition:** Many adults suppress movement in meetings and clinics.  
**Explanation:**
- Restlessness can be quiet or internal.
- No fidgeting in one visit doesn’t rule ADHD out.
**Takeaway:** Looking “still” doesn’t settle the question.

---

## Slide 5 — Overlap is real

**Slide num:** 4 of 5  
**Headline:** Other things  
can look similar  
**Accent:** similar  
**Recognition:** Anxiety. Sleep problems. Stress load.  
**Explanation:**
- Restlessness isn’t ADHD-exclusive.
- Overlap is why lists alone mislead.
**Takeaway:** Similar cues. Different stories.

---

## Slide 6 — What diagnosis actually needs

**Slide num:** 5 of 5  
**Headline:** Diagnosis needs  
more than signs  
**Accent:** more  
**Recognition:** History. Impairment. Pattern over time.  
**Explanation:**
- Childhood-onset clues matter in adult evaluation.
- A clinician looks across settings — not one habit.
**Takeaway:** Physical cues can inform. They don’t decide.

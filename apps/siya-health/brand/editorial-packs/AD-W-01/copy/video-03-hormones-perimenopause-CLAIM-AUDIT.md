## Sources checked (research Step 2 — these four only)

| ID | Paper | What it actually covers on hormones |
|---|---|---|
| **PMC10173330** | Attoe & Climie (2023). *Miss. Diagnosis: A Systematic Review of ADHD in Adult Women.* | Lived experience of undiagnosed ADHD + adult diagnosis (themes: wellbeing, relationships, control, self-acceptance). **No estrogen / dopamine / menopause mechanism content.** |
| **PMC7422602** | Young et al. (2020). *Females with ADHD: expert consensus…* | Consensus: symptoms **may be exacerbated** by hormonal changes in menstrual cycle, pregnancy, menopause. Anecdotal post-menopausal exacerbation. Advises **symptom tracking**; calls for **more research**. |
| **Wiley 2010** | Waite (2010). *Women With ADHD: It Is an Explanation, Not the Excuse Du Jour.* | Recognition, stigma, primary-care role, context-aware intervention. **No hormone / estrogen / dopamine claims in abstract/record.** |
| **PMC4195638** | Quinn & Madhoo (2014). *A review of ADHD in women and girls…* | Hormonal effects on ADHD expression/treatment response are clinically relevant. **Preclinical** estrogen ↔ midbrain dopamine neuron development/plasticity. Endocrine–dopamine interplay in ADHD women **not fully studied**; fluctuations **could be speculated** to modulate impairments. Premenstrual magnification: **≥1 case study**. Follicular vs luteal amphetamine effects in **healthy** women. |

## What we *can* say (supported enough for educational reel)

From **Young 2020** + **Quinn 2014** (with soft language):

1. ADHD in women is often missed or recognized later (all four support under-recognition / late diagnosis themes to varying degrees; Attoe & Waite strongest on lived impact).
2. Some women notice ADHD-like struggles get harder around hormonal life stages (menstrual cycle, pregnancy/postpartum, menopause) — **may** / **some women report**.
3. Researchers are exploring links between **gonadal hormones** and **dopamine** systems involved in ADHD — **early / preclinical / more research needed**.
4. Practical next step: track symptoms across those periods and bring the pattern to a clinician; don’t self-diagnose from a reel.

From **Attoe 2023** + **Waite 2010**: validation that late recognition is common and getting language for your experience matters — **without** hormone mechanism claims.

## REVISED SCRIPT (≈70–80s) — claims-safe

**Working title:** When hormones shift, coping can get harder  
**Primary keyword:** ADHD in women  
**Secondary keyword:** ADHD symptoms menopause *(or)* late diagnosis ADHD women  
**Format:** Talking head + text overlays (calm, eye-level, natural pace)

### Hook (0–3s) — pattern interrupt + relatable

**On-screen:** “It wasn’t sudden.”  
**VO:** “If things got harder in your thirties or forties… it wasn’t sudden.”

### Body

**VO / on-screen beats:**

“Many women with ADHD weren’t the ‘hyper little kid’ stereotype.  
So the struggle stayed quiet for years — good grades, people-pleasing, last-minute panic that somehow worked.

Then life got heavier. Pregnancy. Postpartum. Cycle shifts. Menopause.  
And the same coping tricks stopped covering as much.

That doesn’t automatically mean ‘hormones caused ADHD.’  
ADHD is usually lifelong. What some women notice is this:  
**symptoms can feel louder during hormonal life stages.**

Researchers and clinicians are still studying how sex hormones interact with brain systems involved in ADHD — including dopamine pathways.  
The science is early. The lived experience is not.

So if your focus, overwhelm, or emotional load changed around those transitions — you’re not inventing it.  
Track what you notice. Bring that timeline to a clinician who understands ADHD in women.”

### Close (last 8–10s)

**On-screen:** “Ask. Don’t self-diagnose.”  
**VO:** “This is education, not a diagnosis. If this sounds familiar, talk with a clinician about adult ADHD evaluation.”  
**CTA:** Link in bio → siya.health ADHD care / hormones-women blog.

### Spoken word count target
~145–165 words · calm · one breath per idea · no “chemical buffer,” no serotonin claim, no HRT pitch.

## Caption (revised)

**ADHD in women** is often missed when childhood looked “fine” on the outside.

Some women later notice focus, overwhelm, or emotional load getting harder around **hormonal life stages** — like after pregnancy or during menopause. That doesn’t prove hormones “created” ADHD. It *can* mean symptoms feel harder to mask when life and biology both shift.

Researchers are still studying how sex hormones relate to ADHD-related brain systems. What we can say today: your timeline matters. Track patterns. Ask a clinician about adult ADHD evaluation if the labels never quite fit.

## Caption

Adult ADHD shows up in relationships in specific, nameable ways — not as a vague "attention problem."

Time blindness makes reliability genuinely harder, not optional. Rejection sensitive dysphoria (RSD) can turn ordinary feedback into disproportionate shame. Hyperfocus cycles can look like emotional withdrawal to a partner, even when nothing has changed internally. And limerence — sudden, intense infatuation — is a documented, dopamine-driven pattern in ADHD; feeling it isn't a character failure, though how someone acts on it still matters.

None of these are excuses. They're patterns. And naming a pattern out loud — "that's my RSD talking" — is often the first step to actually managing it as a couple.

What helps in practice: pausing before reacting, replacing memory with shared systems, splitting the mental load fairly, and treating symptoms as something to manage together rather than a verdict on the relationship.

## Caption

ADHD doesn't ruin love — but it does rewrite how love feels day to day. Here's a field guide to four patterns that show up a lot, and what actually helps.

→ Time blindness isn't disrespect. It's a brain wiring difference that makes "I'll be there in 5" genuinely unreliable.

→ Rejection sensitivity (RSD) can turn a small comment into a wave of shame — fast, and out of proportion to the moment.

→ That sudden, intense crush you can't explain? It's called limerence — a dopamine-driven pattern that's common with ADHD. Feeling it doesn't make you a bad partner. What you choose to do with it is what matters.

→ Hyperfocus, then distance? That's an attention cycle, not a caring cycle — but it still lands hard on the person waiting for you to "come back."

None of these are character flaws. They're patterns — and naming them out loud is the first step to actually changing them.

What helps: pause before reacting, shared lists instead of memory, a fair split of the mental load, and saying the pattern's name when it shows up ("that's my RSD talking").

Educational only. Not a diagnosis or a substitute for therapy.

## Caption

You don’t want a peptide.

You want your body back.

That’s the real product being sold when the internet calls BPC-157 the “Wolverine peptide” — tendons, muscles, gut, joints, recovery, all in one vial.

Here’s the honest tension:

The preclinical biology is genuinely interesting. Animal and lab work has explored blood-vessel and tissue-repair pathways. Interesting science is not the same as proven treatment.

Most of the wow is still rats, mice, and cells.

The human file? Tiny uncontrolled pilots. One widely discussed safety report involved two people. Useful as a starting point. Not proof of Wolverine.

**Practical step:** Pause long enough to ask what is proven in humans for *your* problem — then Talk to a Clinician before buying anything pitched as healing in a vial.

Part 2 drops next: what to ask before you buy (FDA status, product quality, athlete rules).

Educational only. Not medical advice. Not a recommendation to use or avoid BPC-157 for any individual. Associations and animal data ≠ approved therapy.

# LinkedIn Company — LONGEVITY-2026-07-24-bpc157-hope-vs-proof (Part 1)

BPC-157 is not a “scam vs science” story.

It is a hope-vs-proof story.

Preclinical work on tissue-repair pathways is interesting enough to respect. The human evidence base remains tiny and largely uncontrolled. One widely discussed safety pilot involved two adults. A registered Phase 2 trial is not the same as published efficacy.

For clinicians and patients: promising biology does not authorize confident recovery claims.

**Practical step:** Ask what outcome is proven in humans for this person’s problem — then decide with a clinician, not a discount code.

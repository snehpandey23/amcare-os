Sometimes the diagnosis isn't wrong.
It's just not the whole picture.

Three experiences people often describe — shared here as composites, not patient stories:

• "I was told it was depression." Therapy helped. Something still didn't make sense.
• "My brain has always felt like a tree." One thought becomes another… then another.
• "I was told it wasn't ADHD." At 65, the picture stayed confusing.

Different conditions can look alike. Depression, anxiety, trauma, and ADHD can overlap — and sometimes more than one is present.

The goal isn't to collect diagnoses. It's to understand what's actually causing your symptoms.

If "something still doesn't add up" sounds familiar after years of trying, a structured, whole-person evaluation can help — including by confirming it is *not* ADHD.

Practical step: Bring the full history (attention, mood, anxiety, trauma, sleep) to a qualified clinician rather than stopping at the first label that partly fits.

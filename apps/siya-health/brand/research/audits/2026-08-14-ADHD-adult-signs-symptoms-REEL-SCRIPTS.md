## AD-SS-03 — Handbrake on

**Practical change:** Name the one area (work/home/relationships) where it shows up most; track for a week before assuming it's just stress.

**Script**
- Hook (0–3s): **Handbrake on.** — figure walking forward, faint trailing brake-line.
- Beat 1 (3–10s): **You want to move. Something's holding you back.** — inbox/laundry/mail flicker, each heavier than it should be.
- Beat 2 (10–17s): **It's not laziness. It's not a lack of trying.** — figure straining forward, still moving.
- Close (17–24s): **If this has been lifelong, it's worth naming — not just pushing through.** → siya.health/answers/signs-of-adult-adhd · Educational only

**Claim-check:** All LOW — metaphor + lived feeling, explicit non-diagnosis framing, no stats.

## AD-SS-02 — Phone in my hand. Nothing gets done.

**Practical change:** Write 3 lifelong examples across work + home this week; bring them plus a sleep note to a clinician instead of a checklist alone.

**Script**
- Hook (0–3s): **Phone in my hand. Nothing gets done.** — hand holding phone, glowing untouched task list behind.
- Beat 1 (3–10s): **I want to start. I just… can't.** — time-lapse light change showing hours passing, same pose.
- Beat 2 (10–18s): **Sometimes it feels physical — like something's holding the switch down.** — soft illustrated "weight" metaphor, not literal medical imagery.
- Beat 3 (18–25s): **"I match most of these signs… but I'm also up all night with bad sleep. What now?"** — sleep/mood can look like this too; that's what an evaluation sorts out.
- Close (25–32s): **Write down 3 examples. Bring them to a clinician — not just a checklist.** → siya.health/answers/signs-of-adult-adhd · Educational only

**Claim-check:** All LOW — direct paraphrase of the real YT comment question; no "you have ADHD" conclusion.

## AD-SS-09 — 12 things adult ADHD can look like

**Practical change:** Circle the 3–4 signs that feel most true and most impairing; bring that shortlist (not the whole list) to a clinician.

**Script**
- Hook (0–2s): **12 things adult ADHD can look like.** — quiet cream background, quiet logo mark.
- Beats (2–28s, ~2s each): acting fast/thinking later · disorganized · can't prioritize · poor time sense · losing focus · bad at multitasking · restless even sitting still · poor planning · frustrated over small things · mood swings · unfinished tasks everywhere · short fuse/hard to cope with stress.
- Close (28–34s): **Matching a few of these? That's a reason to look closer — not a diagnosis.** / **A real evaluation checks the whole pattern, not just today's list.** → siya.health/answers/signs-of-adult-adhd · Educational only

**Claim-check:** All 12 items sourced directly from NIMH (2024) + Gentile et al. (2006) — no invented symptoms, no score/threshold gimmick. Highest scrutiny pack — never publish beats-only cut without the differential close (see medical-flags.md — cross-checked against TikTok ADHD misinformation study PMC9659797).

## AD-SS-04 — My brain never clocks out

**Practical change:** Log a week of never-fully-resting moments and how they affect sleep/relationships; bring the log to an evaluation.

**Script**
- Hook (0–3s): **My brain never clocks out.** — woman lying still in bed, eyes open, faint task-thought-bubbles drifting above.
- Beat 1 (3–12s): **Even rest feels like the next item on a list.** — daily scenes each overlaid with a faint ticking task icon.
- Beat 2 (12–20s): **This isn't hyperactivity you can see. It's the kind no one notices.** — calm outward, cluttered inward contrast.
- Close (20–28s): **Women's ADHD often looks like this — not louder, just quieter and exhausting.** → siya.health/blog/adhd-in-women · Educational only

**Claim-check:** LOW — matches NIMH's "inner restlessness" description; no misdiagnosis percentage used.

## AD-SS-05 — Started A. Finished neither.

**Practical change:** One task, visible timer, phone in another room.

**Script**
- Hook (0–3s): **I started cleaning one shelf.** — cheerful energy, single shelf.
- Beat 1 (3–12s): **Four hours later…** — shelf spotless, a *different* room now torn apart mid-project, rest of house untouched.
- Beat 2 (12–20s): **Started the report. Brain said "organize your desk first." Neither got done.** — split-screen half-document/half-desk, both abandoned.
- Close (20–28s): **It's not a discipline problem. It's how attention moves.** / **Try this: one task, visible timer, phone in another room.** → siya.health/answers/executive-dysfunction-adhd · Educational only

**Claim-check:** LOW — humor-safe, no completion-rate stat, concrete practical change.

## AD-SS-10 — I thought I was just bad at life

**Practical change:** Swap one "I'm just bad at ___" thought this week for a specific name (e.g., "task initiation is hard right now").

**Script**
- Hook (0–3s): **I used to think I was just bad at life.** — figure surrounded by soft everyday "failures" (unpaid bill, missed call, unfolded laundry).
- Beat 1 (3–10s): **Everyone else seemed to manage. So it had to be me, right?** — figure watching others move smoothly.
- Beat 2 (10–17s): **Turns out "bad at life" had a name — and it wasn't a character flaw.** — lighting shifts, figure stands taller.
- Close (17–22s): **If this sounds familiar, it might be worth a real conversation — not another label you gave yourself.** → siya.health/blog/youre-not-lazy-signs-undiagnosed-adult-adhd · Educational only

**Claim-check:** LOW — self-blame reframe without asserting diagnosis; coordinate wording with AD-S-01 so hooks don't collide.

## AD-SS-07 — Late for everything, including adulting

**Practical change:** Add a 15-minute buffer to every time estimate for a week; compare estimate vs. actual.

**Script**
- Hook (0–3s): **Late for work. Late for dinner. Late for things with no deadline.** — clock hands spinning faster than the figure can move.
- Beat 1 (3–11s): **"Just leave earlier," they said.** — earlier alarm set, still walks out at the same time, confused.
- Beat 2 (11–18s): **It's not about trying harder. Time just... doesn't land the same way.** — stretched, wobbly clock face (not literal medical imagery).
- Close (18–24s): **Try this: add 15 minutes to every estimate for a week. Compare guess vs. reality.** → siya.health/answers/time-blindness-adhd · Educational only

**Claim-check:** LOW — non-shaming, no circadian/mechanism claim (that's pinned as separate research in the mining audit).

## AD-SS-06 — Hyperfocus without a steering wheel

**Practical change:** Set a hard external alarm before starting an absorbing task.

**Script**
- Hook (0–3s): **Hyperfocus is real. It's also a trap.** — figure absorbed in a task, warm glow radiating outward.
- Beat 1 (3–12s): **Missed the text. Missed lunch. Missed the meeting.** — notifications piling up unseen.
- Beat 2 (12–19s): **The focus isn't the problem. Aiming it is.** — glow flickers between three tasks.
- Close (19–25s): **Try this: set an alarm before you start. Hyperfocus won't stop itself.** → siya.health/blog/adhd-symptoms-overlooked · Educational only

**Claim-check:** LOW–MEDIUM — framed as "some people," not a universal ADHD rule; no neurochemistry claim.

## AD-SS-08 — I thought it was just depression

**Practical change:** Bring the specific cycle (overwhelm → shutdown → guilt → repeat) — not just "I feel depressed" — to your evaluation.

**Script**
- Hook (0–3s): **I thought it was just depression.** — figure sitting still, tired, evening light.
- Beat 1 (3–13s): **I held it together all day. Work. Parenting. Everyone's needs but mine.** — quick flashes of juggling tasks, smiling through calls.
- Beat 2 (13–22s): **Then the next day, I just... couldn't.** — same figure collapsed, unable to start anything.
- Close (22–30s): **Sometimes what looks like depression is years of compensating. Worth untangling with someone who can look at both.** → siya.health/answers/late-adhd-diagnosis-adults · Educational only

**Claim-check:** MEDIUM — heaviest tone in the batch; review closely. No comorbidity %, no "ADHD causes depression" claim, no crisis/safety content (route to crisis language elsewhere if tone drifts — see medical-flags.md).

## Plain-language summary (Gentile 2006)

ADHD can continue into adulthood; adult presentation often shows less overt hyperactivity and more problems starting tasks, sustaining effort, organizing/prioritizing, and managing frustration. Diagnosis requires childhood continuity and impairment across settings. Mood, anxiety, substance use, personality disorders, and medical conditions (e.g., thyroid, sleep apnea) belong on the differential. Treatment discussion includes stimulants, non-stimulants, and psychotherapy.

## Educational points usable after medical flags (NIMH 2024)

- Adults can have ADHD; symptoms must impair function in multiple areas.
- Presentations: inattentive, hyperactive-impulsive, or combined.
- Adult diagnosis still requires earlier-life symptom evidence.
- Women/girls often missed in childhood.
- Treatment may include medication, psychotherapy, coaching, lifestyle supports.
- Sleep problems are common in adults with ADHD (**specific % → claims register**).

## Step 1–2 — Source base (validated)

### Informational / clinical
- Adult ADHD symptoms: restlessness, fidgeting, difficulty sitting still, subtle adult hyperactivity (Mayo, Cleveland Clinic, ADDA)
- European consensus: adult hyperactivity often = *inner* restlessness, fidgeting with muscle strain while “sitting still,” restless sleep — calm presentation during assessment does **not** exclude ADHD
- Sleep comorbidity high (~60%+ screen positive in clinical samples): delayed sleep phase, insomnia, RLS/PLMD-type symptoms
- Inattentive presentation: ADHD can exist with little/no overt physical hyperactivity

### Reddit (community — high signal, not diagnostic)
| Thread theme | Engagement signal | Takeaway |
|--------------|-------------------|----------|
| Visible / physical ADHD symptoms | Very high (500+ upvotes) | Leg bounce, nail biting, pacing, posture shifts, “can’t sit through a movie” |
| Body / physical ADHD rarely discussed | High (~99 up / 72 comments) | Sleep, proprioception, clumsiness, sensory, chronic tension — lived body experience |
| Physical-ADHD content “overhyped” pushback | Low upvotes, active comments | Community self-corrects: not every movement = ADHD |
| Can clinicians “see” ADHD on you? | Mid comment volume | Masking; diagnosis is history + impairment, not a glance |
| What is fidgeting *like*? | High (~99 / 97) | Fidgeting as focus tool, not only nervous habit |
| ADHD vs anxiety restlessness | Smaller threads | Different drivers: stimulation-seeking vs threat/worry |

### Cross-platform
- **Instagram:** Influencer/education reels list physical “tells” (fidget, leg shake, restless sleep) — often listicle, thin on differential
- **Facebook:** Same short-form shares circulating
- **Blogs/press:** “Surprising signs” lists exist; rarely say clearly “these are not diagnostic criteria”
- **Stop rule:** Not triggered — ≥2–3 real findings per platform

### Observer note (editorial)
Physical signs (fidgeting, restlessness, leg shaking, sleep problems) are commonly reported and clinically recognized as *hyperactivity/restlessness expressions*, but they are **not** sure-shot diagnostic criteria. Overlap with anxiety, sleep disorders, RLS, stimulants/caffeine, thyroid, iron deficiency, etc. Best content lane: **myth-breaking recognition** — share what’s commonly seen, then refuse false certainty.

## Common questions (audience language)

1. Does ADHD show up in the body, or is it only “in the mind”?
2. Why do people with ADHD fidget / bounce their legs so much?
3. Can you have ADHD if you’re not hyperactive or fidgety?
4. Can other people (or a psychiatrist) *see* that I have ADHD?
5. Is my restlessness ADHD or anxiety — or both?
6. Are sleep problems / restless legs part of ADHD or something else?
7. Do these physical habits mean I should self-diagnose?

## Common misconceptions

| Myth | Reality (awareness-safe) |
|------|--------------------------|
| ADHD always looks like visible hyperactivity | Adults often show *inner* restlessness; inattentive ADHD may have little overt movement |
| Fidgeting = ADHD | Fidgeting is common in ADHD but also anxiety, boredom, habit, stimulants — not diagnostic alone |
| If someone sits still in an appointment, they don’t have ADHD | Masking / muscle strain / temporary stillness ≠ absence of ADHD |
| Physical signs can confirm ADHD by themselves | Diagnosis needs lifelong pattern, impairment across settings, clinical assessment — not a symptom checklist selfie |
| ADHD restlessness and anxiety restlessness are the same | Drivers differ (stimulation/regulation vs threat/worry) and often co-occur |
| “Physical ADHD” content is either all true or all fake | Nuance: body expressions are real *and* overhyped when sold as proof |

## Emotional patterns

- **Validation hunger** — “Finally someone named the leg bounce / can’t-sit-still feeling.”
- **Shame & self-policing** — years of being told to stop fidgeting; fear of looking unprofessional.
- **Doubt / impostor ADHD** — “If I’m not bouncing off walls, maybe it’s not real.”
- **Fear of over-identifying** — Reddit pushback against TikTok-style overclaiming; audience wants credibility.
- **Confusion at the overlap** — anxiety, sleep, RLS, burnout all feel similar in the body.
- **Relief when nuance appears** — “Common, not conclusive” lands better than “10 signs you definitely have ADHD.”

## (1) Themes that repeat across multiple sources

1. **Adult hyperactivity is quieter** — fidgeting, pacing, doodling, leg shake, inner motor — not childhood running.
2. **Fidgeting as regulation** — movement often helps attention; not only “nervous energy.”
3. **Sleep body signs** — delayed sleep, restless sleep, RLS-like leg urge appear repeatedly in clinical + lived accounts.
4. **Masking makes ADHD hard to “see”** — others (and sometimes clinicians in a short visit) may miss it.
5. **You can have ADHD without obvious physical hyperactivity** — inattentive presentation is real and under-recognized.
6. **Overlap / differential is the missing half of most viral lists** — anxiety, sleep disorders, iron/thyroid, etc.
7. **Community both amplifies and critiques** “physical signs” content — appetite for lists + appetite for myth-breaks.

---

## (2) Content gap

**Gap:** Viral and press content lists physical “tells,” but almost never teaches adults **how to hold those signs without self-diagnosing** — i.e. *common body expressions ≠ diagnostic proof*, plus a simple differential frame (ADHD restlessness vs anxiety vs sleep/RLS) and a next step (pattern + impairment → screening/clinician).

Siya already covers “not lazy,” women missed, iron fog, ADHD vs anxiety at the *symptom-label* level. What’s thin: a dedicated **body-first, myth-safe** piece for 25–50 adults that answers “does ADHD have physical signs?” without becoming another diagnostic listicle.

## (3) Ranked content angles (tied to awareness goal)

### Rank 1 — Primary pack (`AD-P-01`) — Carousel + Blog spine
**Angle:** “Physical signs that *may* show up with ADHD — and why they’re not enough to diagnose.”  
**Why #1:** Matches audience question + your observation + brand safety; fills the gap; works as carousel structure (sign → what it can mean → what it doesn’t prove).  
**Formats:** Carousel (lead) · Blog (depth) · Reel cut-down  
**Practical change:** If these patterns are lifelong and impairing, take a validated screener / talk to a clinician — don’t diagnose from fidgeting alone.  
**Emotion:** Relieved + Curious  
**Avoid:** “X physical sign = you have ADHD.”

### Rank 2 — Reel hook (same insight, sharper entry)
**Angle:** “We’ve all heard ADHD symptoms… but does ADHD show physical signs on the body?”  
**Why #2:** Strong stop-scroll question for Reels; funnels to Rank 1 carousel/blog.  
**Formats:** Reel (15–30s) → CTA to carousel/blog  
**Beat:** Hook → 2–3 common body expressions → “common ≠ conclusive” → overlap line → next step  
**Emotion:** Curious

### Rank 3 — Comparison deep-dive (blog section or follow-up carousel)
**Angle:** “ADHD restlessness vs anxiety restlessness — same body, different driver.”  
**Why #3:** High clinical value and differentiation from generic listicles; supports awareness without acquisition hard-sell; can extend Rank 1.  
**Formats:** Blog section inside Rank 1, or secondary carousel `AD-P-02`  
**Practical change:** Notice *when* the restlessness shows up (boredom/understimulation vs worry/threat) — bring both patterns to a clinician if needed.  
**Emotion:** Validated + Clearer  
**Note:** Existing `adhd-vs-anxiety` spoke — cross-link; don’t duplicate entire piece.

## Spine thesis

**COVID didn’t give most adults ADHD. It took away the scaffolding that hid it — and raised the self-management demands overnight.**

## Two stories (keep separate)

| Story | Use in |
|-------|--------|
| **A. Context collapse** (routines, remote school/work, EF stress test) | Parts 1–2 spine |
| **B. Infection / Long COVID fog** | Caption caveat only — not Part 1 spine |

## Safe claims
- ADHD tightly linked to EF; many people rely on external structure
- Lockdowns removed scaffolds while increasing self-regulation demands
- Online school/work assumed multi-platform self-management
- ADHD teens had more remote-learning difficulty; routines protective
- Adult ADHD-related impairment / help-seeking rose under remote conditions
- ASRS working-adult estimate ~4.4% → ~9.3% in one U.S. pandemic study (not global prevalence)
- Some people benefited when environments were adapted

## Avoid
- “Everyone got ADHD after COVID”
- COVID *causes* lifelong adult ADHD
- DIY stimulant protocols for Long COVID
- Infection as the only explanation for diagnosis surge

## Practical change
Rebuild 3 external scaffolds for 14 days (fixed wake · one protected work/school block · one accountability check-in) → note what improves → Talk to a Clinician / Book ADHD Evaluation if lifelong pattern + impairment.

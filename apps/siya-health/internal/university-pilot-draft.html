Siya Health · Internal

University / college health partnership — concept draft

Audience: Rebecca (internal review) · Status: early concept · Pricing & utilization assumptions are directional, not locked · Related model: California employer pilot (reference only)

The concept

A campus health partnership that would offer Siya Health’s cognitive / psychiatric monitoring and care access to college students. Framing under discussion: a safety net and early-detection option for a population that is often outside parents’ insurance coverage and underserved by campus mental health capacity.

This is an idea we want challenge on — not a claim that demand or unit economics are proven.

Proposed structure (draft, not finalized)

  • Base plan: roughly $19–20 per student per month, paid by the university (not by individual students) for enrolled participants.
  • Includes: regular Creyos-based cognitive / psychiatric monitoring (attention, memory, executive function, mood screening); provider-approved wellness guidance; one annual comprehensive physical / consultation.
  • À la carte (billed separately from the base plan): routine visits (~$25–30 — e.g. acne, minor illness); ongoing condition-management pathways (~$100/month — e.g. ADHD diagnosis and treatment).
  • Staffing at meaningful scale: e.g. ~10,000 enrolled students — base-plan revenue would be intended to support a dedicated, fixed-salary provider (same economic shape as the California employer pilot), rather than classic per-visit reimbursement economics.
  • Self-monitoring + Spruce between formal visits (key differentiator): students track their own data and reach the care team in Spruce — secure messaging, a quick photo of a concern, or video when needed — without waiting for an appointment slot to ask a quick question. Siya would not proactively interpret every data point; a licensed provider responds when a student reaches out. Share what’s going on and hear back the same day; this is fast communication, not automated diagnosis. CarePatron stays the scheduling / billing / documentation backend for the annual physical and any formally booked visits.

How students would actually reach the care team

For a lower-acuity, high-volume campus population, most enrolled students are expected to be light utilizers. The operational bet is that day-to-day contact lives in Spruce (async by default), while CarePatron is used for intake booking, the annual comprehensive visit, and any à-la-carte scheduled consultations — not as the primary patient chat surface.

Open questions — not yet resolved

These are explicit unknowns. Do not treat them as closed when reading this draft.

Student demand is unverified

Would students actually value or engage with this, or is it a benefit universities buy that goes largely unused? No student survey or demand-testing has been done yet.

Utilization assumptions are unconfirmed

The fixed-cost provider math only works if usage stays proportionally low across a large enrolled population. That has been reasoned through directionally — not modeled with real numbers.

Backup provider coverage is not planned

Backup coverage is not yet designed for this concept or for the employer model.

Basic-plan pricing vs. real visit cost is unchecked

The $19–20 base and the add-on consultation prices have not been stress-tested against real per-visit cost the way the employer pilot standing price ($100) was.

Reference point

This mirrors the employer pilot’s core proposition — one dedicated care relationship, with combined primary / psychiatric / cognitive care under a single institutional price, rather than fragmented single-purpose apps — but adapted for a lower-acuity, high-volume population where most enrolled users are expected to be low utilizers. Spruce-first messaging is part of that adaptation: students should not need an appointment slot to ask a quick question. That adaptation is still the risky part: the employer math and the campus math are not the same until utilization and demand are tested.

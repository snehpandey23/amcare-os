What a prescription visit includes

A documented visit with a licensed clinician. Not a checkout.

  • History and current medicines

    The clinician reviews symptoms, medicines you already take, and why a prescription is being considered. That review happens before anything is sent to a pharmacy.

  • A real encounter

    The visit is documented. If a prescription is appropriate, it is sent electronically to a pharmacy you choose. A prescription is not guaranteed.

  • What the visit can be for

    Acute problems that telehealth can handle, such as common infections and allergies. Men’s health, including hormone evaluation and erectile dysfunction, when clinically indicated. Refills when they are part of an established plan.

State and prescribing limits

Licensure, the kind of medicine, and follow-up all limit what can be prescribed.

  • Where you are

    Care is available in California, Texas, Pennsylvania, and Florida. The clinician must be licensed in the state where you are at the time of the visit. Eligibility is confirmed at scheduling.

  • Controlled medicines

    Some medicines, including ADHD stimulants, are controlled substances. They are not ordered from a quiz or an anonymous site. Expect an established clinician relationship, identity verification, prescription-monitoring checks where the state requires them, and follow-up. State and federal rules apply.

  • Not a guarantee

    Availability depends on your state, the clinical picture, and prescribing rules. The pharmacy bills the medicine separately. Hospital care is not part of this telehealth visit.

How a refill works

  • An existing plan

    A refill is a review of a medicine you are already using in a care plan, not a new order with no history. The clinician decides whether continuing it is appropriate.

  • Pharmacy rules

    The prescription goes to your pharmacy. Controlled medicines can have early-refill limits or supply limits. A shipping delay is not a reason to use someone else’s medicine.

  • Follow-up

    Ongoing medicines stay on a follow-up schedule. If something has changed — a side effect, a new medicine, or a missed visit — say so before asking for a refill. For emergencies, call 911.
